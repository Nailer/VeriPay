This quickstart walks through the **end-to-end money path** of VeriPay against the live testnet deployment: claiming a seller handle, paying through it by card, observing the escrow lock, releasing funds, and independently verifying every step on-chain.

<Accordion title="In plain words" icon="lightbulb">
  You're going to pretend to be a shop owner and a customer at the same time. You'll make a payment link, pay through it with a fake card, watch the money get locked safely, then release it — and finally check for yourself that it all really happened.
</Accordion>

<TestnetNote />

## What you need

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Two browsers or devices" icon="smartphone">
    A seller **cannot pay their own link**, so use two devices, or two browser profiles, or one normal + one private window.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Biometrics (recommended)" icon="fingerprint">
    Face ID, Touch ID, an Android fingerprint or Windows Hello. Without it, use **Other ways to sign in** (email, Google, wallet).
  </Card>
  <Card title="The Paystack test card" icon="credit-card">
    `4084 0840 8408 4081`, any future expiry date, CVV `408`. It's Paystack's public test card — it charges nothing real.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Accordion title="Why two devices?">
  The server rejects an intent whose `buyerAddress` equals the seller's `address` (`"You can't pay your own link."`), and the contract itself reverts with `buyer cannot be the seller`. A passkey account is deterministic per device-held passkey, so the same browser profile will produce the same account for both roles.

  **In plain words:** VeriPay won't let you pay yourself, so you need a second "person". Another phone, another browser, or a private window works.
</Accordion>

## Part 1 — Be the seller

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the seller page">
    On device **A**, go to [veripay.store/sell](https://veripay.store/sell) and tap **Get your payment link**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign in with your fingerprint">
    Tap the fingerprint / Face ID button. The browser runs a **WebAuthn ceremony with the PRF extension**; the 32-byte PRF output becomes BIP-39 entropy, which is derived along `m/44'/60'/0'/0/0` into your account key — entirely on your device.
    <Accordion title="In plain words">
      Your phone creates a secure "passkey" protected by your fingerprint. From that passkey, VeriPay's page builds your account right there on your phone. No password, no 12-word phrase to write down. Use the same passkey later and you get the same account back.
    </Accordion>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a shop name and link name">
    Enter a shop name (2–60 characters) and a link name (3–30 characters: lowercase letters, numbers, `-` or `_`, matching `^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9_-]{2,29}$`). Some names are reserved (`admin`, `pay`, `sell`, `veripay`, …).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign the claim">
    Your account signs the exact message `VeriPay: I am claiming the payment link "<handle>" for <address>.` The server recovers the signer with `verifyMessage` and stores the handle only if it matches.
    <Accordion title="In plain words">
      You approve a short sentence that says "this link belongs to me". VeriPay checks the approval really came from you before saving your link. This stops anyone from creating a link that would send your customers' money somewhere else.
    </Accordion>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy your link">
    You now have `veripay.store/pay/<your-handle>` with **Copy link** and **Share on WhatsApp** buttons. Leave this page open — new orders appear here.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Part 2 — Be the buyer

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the link on device B">
    Paste the link into the second browser. You'll see the shop name and the seller's **record**: paid out, disputes, refunds. A brand-new shop shows zeros.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the order">
    Enter an amount in naira (minimum ₦100, maximum ₦5,000,000), what you're buying (at least 3 characters), and an email for the receipt.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign in and pay">
    Tap **Pay ₦… safely**, sign in with your fingerprint, and complete the Paystack popup with the test card.
    <Accordion title="What happens on the server">
      1. `POST /api/pay/intent` creates a `pay_intents` row in status `awaiting_payment` and returns a reference `VP-<intentId>-<timestamp36>`.
      2. The Paystack inline popup charges the card **under that reference**.
      3. `POST /api/pay/confirm` checks the reference prefix belongs to this intent, calls Paystack `GET /transaction/verify/:reference` with the secret key, and checks `status = success`, `currency = NGN` and `amount ≥ expected − ₦1`.
      4. The intent is claimed with `UPDATE … WHERE status = 'awaiting_payment'`, a single delivery slot is taken (`mint_tx = 'pending'`), and exactly the paid amount of vNGN is minted to you.
      5. If your MON balance is below 0.08, the server sends you 0.15 MON for network fees (max 3 top-ups per payment).

      **In plain words:** VeriPay asks the card company "did this person really pay, and how much?". Only after a "yes" does it give you the same amount in digital naira plus a little fuel for the network — and it's careful to never do this twice for one payment.
    </Accordion>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch it lock">
    Your device signs two transactions — `approve(escrow, amount)` on vNGN and `createTradeWithToken(seller, item, vNGN, amount)` on the escrow — without any wallet pop-up. You get a receipt: order number, reference, status **Held until you confirm delivery**, and a line like *"Secured in 1.1s · network fee ₦0.72, paid for you"*.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Part 3 — Deliver and release

<Steps>
  <Step title="Seller sees the order">
    Back on device A, the order appears under **Your orders** as **Paid — deliver this order**. If you enabled device alerts, you also got a push notification.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Buyer confirms delivery">
    On device B, open **My orders** → the order → **I received my order — pay the seller**. This calls `releaseToSeller(id)`; the contract pays the seller 99% and books 1% into `accruedFees[vNGN]`.
    <Accordion title="In plain words">
      The customer says "I got it", and the seller is paid immediately. VeriPay keeps 1% as its fee — but only because the sale went through.
    </Accordion>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the seller's record">
    Open [veripay.store/seller](https://veripay.store/seller) and type the seller's link name. **Paid out** has gone up by one. That number was recomputed from on-chain events.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Part 4 — Verify it yourself

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the proof page">
    On any receipt, tap **View proof on Monad**. The `/proof/<txHash>` page fetches the transaction receipt and block **directly from a Monad RPC node** (not VeriPay's database), decodes the escrow's event logs, and describes them in words.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cross-check on the explorer">
    Follow the explorer link, or open the escrow on [Monadscan](https://testnet.monadscan.com/address/0x00bdf9fbc9f59cc6814bbc7a91b19bbad1517e6d) and find your `TradeCreated` and `FundsReleased` events.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Query the indexer (optional)">
    ```bash
    curl -s https://indexer.dev.hyperindex.xyz/09eb2b1/v1/graphql \
      -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
      -d '{"query":"{ AddressReputation(limit: 5) { id totalTrades completedTrades disputedTrades refundedTrades } }"}'
    ```
    <Accordion title="In plain words">
      This is an optional, more technical check: it asks the public "index" of all VeriPay orders for the latest seller records. You'll see the same numbers the app shows.
    </Accordion>
  </Step>
</Steps>
