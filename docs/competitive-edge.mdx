### Why Not Just Use PayPal?

PayPal and similar services have **severe limitations in Africa**:

- **Restricted in Nigeria** — PayPal does not allow Nigerians to receive payments
- **High fees** — 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction, plus currency conversion fees
- **Buyer bias** — PayPal's dispute system heavily favors buyers, leaving sellers vulnerable
- **Bank dependency** — Requires a bank account and credit card in a region where 60%+ are unbanked
- **Slow settlements** — Funds can be held for days or weeks during disputes

### Why Not Traditional Escrow?

Traditional escrow companies like Escrow.com are designed for high-value Western transactions:

- **Not available** in most African countries
- **Minimum transaction amounts** often start at $100+
- **Extensive KYC** — passport, utility bills, bank statements required
- **Days of waiting** — transactions take 3–7 business days to settle
- **Expensive** — 5–10% fees eat into the transaction value

### Why Not Other DeFi Protocols?

Existing DeFi escrow protocols miss the mark for the African market:

- **Built for DeFi natives** — complex UIs that assume crypto expertise
- **No social login** — require users to already have a wallet and tokens
- **No communication layer** — no way for buyer/seller to discuss the trade
- **No notification system** — users must manually check for updates
- **Not localized** — designed for Western/global markets, not Africa-specific use cases
