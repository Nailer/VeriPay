## Commerce without a checkout

A large share of online retail in Nigeria does not happen in online stores. It happens in **Instagram DMs, WhatsApp chats and Jiji listings**, between people who have never met. Discovery is social; payment is a bank transfer to a stranger's account number; there is no checkout, no chargeback and no intermediary.

<Accordion title="In plain words" icon="lightbulb">
  Most people shop online by messaging a seller on Instagram or WhatsApp and sending money by bank transfer. There's no "shop" website protecting either side.
</Accordion>

## The standoff

Every one of those sales begins with the same **coordination failure**:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="The seller says: pay first" icon="store">
    Sellers have shipped goods and watched buyers reverse transfers or simply vanish.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The buyer says: deliver first" icon="shopping-bag">
    Buyers have paid and been blocked, sent an empty box, or sent a fake.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Somebody has to move first, and that party bears **100% of the counterparty risk**. In game-theory terms the dominant strategy for a dishonest actor is to defect after the other side commits, and the honest side has no enforcement mechanism.

<Accordion title="In plain words">
  Whoever goes first takes all the risk. A scammer just waits for the other person to pay or ship, then disappears. The honest person has no way to get their money or goods back.
</Accordion>

## The question nobody can answer

Before paying, every buyer asks the same thing: **"How do I know you're legit?"** Today the answer is a screenshot of past "testimonials" — trivially fabricated, impossible to verify. So:

- Buyers lose money to sellers who vanish.
- **Honest sellers lose sales**, because to a buyer they look identical to scammers.
- Small sellers can't build trust that travels with them across platforms.

<Accordion title="In plain words">
  There's no reliable way to tell a good seller from a scammer. Screenshots of happy customers can be faked in seconds, so honest sellers suffer too.
</Accordion>

## Why existing escrow never reached these people

Escrow — a neutral party holding funds until delivery — has existed for decades. It never reached Instagram and WhatsApp commerce because every version came with a barrier:

| Approach | Barrier |
|---|---|
| Bank / legal escrow | Requires paperwork, a bank relationship and days to settle; fees make small orders pointless |
| Marketplace escrow (big e-commerce) | Only works inside that marketplace; sellers must list there and accept its fees and rules |
| Crypto escrow dApps | Require a wallet, a seed phrase, a token most Nigerians don't hold, and paying gas |
| "Trusted middleman" on WhatsApp | A person holding the money — who can also disappear |

<Accordion title="In plain words">
  Protection services exist, but they're either slow and expensive, locked inside one big website, too complicated (crypto wallets and secret phrases), or rely on another stranger you'd have to trust.
</Accordion>

## What a solution must do

Derived from the above, any real solution has to:

1. **Hold the money neutrally** — neither buyer, seller nor the platform can take it unilaterally.
2. **Be reachable without crypto knowledge** — naira, a card, a fingerprint.
3. **Be cheap enough for ₦2,000 orders** — the long tail is where most social commerce happens.
4. **Be fast enough to feel like paying** — seconds, not minutes.
5. **Make honesty visible** — a record that can't be faked, bought or edited.
6. **Never strand an honest party** — a silent buyer must not freeze a seller's money forever.
