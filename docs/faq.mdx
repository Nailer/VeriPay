## For everyone

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What is VeriPay, in one sentence?">
    A way to buy from strangers online where your money is held safely until your order arrives — and the seller can prove they're honest with a record nobody can fake.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is this real money?">
    **Not yet.** VeriPay runs on Monad **testnet**. Card payments use Paystack **test mode** and the naira held on-chain is **vNGN**, a test token. Going live requires a security review, a registered business, Paystack live mode, a licensed naira stablecoin or payout partner, and Monad mainnet. See the [Roadmap](/resources/roadmap).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do I need crypto, a wallet, or to understand blockchain?">
    No. You sign in with Face ID / fingerprint, pay with your normal bank card in naira, and VeriPay pays the network fees. You never see a wallet address, a token name or a gas fee on the buying and selling pages.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can VeriPay run away with my money?">
    No — and this is enforced by code, not by promise. The escrow contract has **no function** that lets VeriPay's admin move money held for open orders. The only money VeriPay can withdraw is the fee bucket, which only contains fees from orders that already completed successfully. You can read the [contract source](https://github.com/Nailer/VeriPay/blob/hackathon/metropolis/contracts/VeriPayEscrow.sol) or the [invariants page](/security/invariants).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How much does it cost?">
    **1% of the order, charged to the seller, only when they're paid.** Buyers pay nothing extra. Refunds are free. The network fee (under ₦1) is paid by VeriPay. The fee can never exceed 5% — that limit is hard-coded. See [Fees and pricing](/product/fees-and-pricing).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do I need to download an app?">
    No. VeriPay works in any modern phone browser. You can optionally [install it to your home screen](/guides/install-app) for an app-like experience and notifications.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Which countries is it for?">
    It's designed for Nigerian social commerce first (naira, Paystack, Instagram/WhatsApp/Jiji). The contract accepts any ERC-20 token, including dollar stablecoins, so cross-border use is on the roadmap.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## For buyers

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What happens if the seller never delivers?">
    Don't confirm delivery. Message the seller first; if that doesn't work, tap **Report a problem with this order** *before the confirmation window ends* (7 days). The money is frozen until a reviewer decides. If the seller agrees to refund, you get 100% back.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if I forget to confirm?">
    After 7 days without a confirmation or a problem report, the seller is paid automatically. The order page shows a countdown so you always know how long you have.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I paid, but the page closed before it said 'Payment secured'. Was I charged twice?">
    No. If you paid but the lock didn't finish, just open the seller's link again and sign in. The page finds your earlier payment, **skips the card step**, and finishes locking it. You are never charged twice for the same payment.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do I know the seller's record is real?">
    It's counted from public blockchain events written by the escrow contract for every order. VeriPay doesn't store a score it could edit. Anyone can recount it — see [Reputation](/architecture/reputation).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can a seller fake a good record by buying from themselves?">
    Not trivially: a seller can't pay their own link, and the contract rejects `buyer == seller`. A determined seller could use a second account and pay the 1% fee on each fake order, which makes wash-trading costly but not impossible. Recognising this, VeriPay shows disputes and refunds alongside completions. Stronger sybil resistance is on the roadmap.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I use VeriPay for a Jiji or marketplace purchase?">
    Yes. Ask the seller for their VeriPay link, or check them at [veripay.store/seller](https://veripay.store/seller) first.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## For sellers

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How do I get paid?">
    When the buyer confirms delivery, 99% of the order is released to your account immediately. If the buyer goes silent, you're paid automatically after 7 days. On testnet the balance is test naira and **cannot yet be withdrawn to a bank** — that needs a licensed payout partner.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I change my link name?">
    Not from the app today. Each account can hold exactly one link, and link names are unique. Contact support if you need a change.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can a buyer just report a problem to avoid paying?">
    Reporting a problem doesn't give the buyer the money — it freezes it until a reviewer decides. The reviewer can award any split, including 100% to you. Problem reports also count on the buyer's own record.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why does VeriPay need my signature to create a link?">
    So nobody else can create a link that sends *your* customers' payments to *their* account, or squat a link in your name.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## For developers

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Is the code open source?">
    Yes, MIT-licensed at [github.com/Nailer/VeriPay](https://github.com/Nailer/VeriPay). The `hackathon/metropolis` branch is the current product.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I integrate VeriPay into my own store?">
    Yes. You can send buyers to a seller's `/pay/<handle>` link, read reputation via `GET /api/reputation/:address` or Envio GraphQL, or call `VeriPayEscrow` directly from your own dApp. See the [Integration guide](/developers/integration-guide).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why not use a smart-contract wallet / account abstraction for gas?">
    The passkey account is an EOA derived from the PRF output, so gas is sponsored by a capped server-side MON top-up rather than a paymaster. It's simple and works on any EVM chain today; ERC-4337 / EIP-7702 sponsorship is a reasonable future step.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Where's the Hardhat/Foundry config?">
    There isn't one. Contracts are deployed from Remix and tested with `solc` + an in-process Ganache chain via `node --test` in `contracts/test/`.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why are there two contract versions?">
    v1 (`0xd0cc…1621`) had no fees, disputes or auto-release. v2 (`VeriPayEscrow`) adds them plus ERC-20 support. `src/lib/escrow.ts` detects which is deployed by calling `feeBps()` and normalises both. See [Legacy v1](/contracts/legacy-v1).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## For investors and judges

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Are there real users?">
    Not yet. Every order on the contract was created by the founder's own demo and test wallets. They're real on-chain transactions, but they're not customers.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How does VeriPay make money?">
    A 1% success-only fee on released orders, configurable up to a hard 5% ceiling. A separate naira↔crypto exchange earns a spread (default 3.5%).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Has the contract been audited?">
    No. It has a 30-test local-EVM suite and an end-to-end payment test, but no independent security review. That's the first roadmap item before real money. See [Audit status](/security/audit-status).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why Monad specifically?">
    Locking a payment confirms in ~1 second and costs under ₦1, so VeriPay can protect even ₦2,000 orders and pay the fee itself — and write a permanent event for every order, which is what makes the reputation complete and unfakeable. See [Why Monad](/product/why-monad).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
