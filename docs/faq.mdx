## General

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What is VeriPay?">
    VeriPay is a decentralized escrow protocol built on the Monad blockchain. It allows two parties (a buyer and a seller) to conduct trustless peer-to-peer transactions by locking funds in a smart contract until both parties are satisfied.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is VeriPay free to use?">
    Yes. VeriPay charges **zero platform fees**. The only cost is a minimal Monad gas fee (typically less than $0.01) for on-chain transactions like creating an escrow or releasing funds.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need a crypto wallet to use VeriPay?">
    Not necessarily. You can sign in with your **email, Google account, Apple ID, Facebook, or phone number** — and VeriPay will create a secure, non-custodial wallet for you automatically. If you already have MetaMask, WalletConnect, or another wallet, you can connect that instead.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What blockchain does VeriPay run on?">
    VeriPay runs on **Monad**, a high-performance EVM-compatible Layer 1 blockchain with 10,000+ TPS (transactions per second) and near-zero gas fees. Currently on the Monad Testnet (Chain ID: 10143).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is VeriPay available in my country?">
    VeriPay is a decentralized application accessible from anywhere with internet access. It's designed specifically for the African market (starting with Nigeria/Lagos) but can be used globally. No KYC or geographical restrictions apply.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Security & Trust

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Is my money safe on VeriPay?">
    Yes. Your funds are locked in an **immutable smart contract** on the Monad blockchain. No human — not even VeriPay's team — can access, freeze, or redirect your funds. The contract has no admin keys, no owner function, and no privileged access.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What if the seller doesn't deliver my goods?">
    If the seller fails to deliver, you can request a refund. The process requires the **seller to approve** the refund (a two-step consent model). Use the **in-trade chat** to communicate with the seller and negotiate. If they approve, you can withdraw your MON.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What if the seller refuses to approve a refund?">
    Currently, if the seller refuses, the funds remain locked until the dispute is resolved through communication. **Future versions** will include DAO-based arbitration where neutral third parties can review evidence and make binding decisions.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Has the smart contract been audited?">
    The contract has been internally reviewed and is open source for public inspection. A **professional third-party audit** is planned before mainnet launch. The contract is intentionally simple (6 functions, 1 data structure) to minimize attack surface.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens if VeriPay the company disappears?">
    The smart contract is **immutable and permissionless** — it runs on the Monad blockchain independently. Even if VeriPay's website goes offline, your funds remain accessible through the smart contract. Anyone can build a new frontend that interacts with the same contract.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Using VeriPay

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How do I get MON tokens?">
    **On testnet:** Use the [Monad Faucet](https://faucet.monad.xyz) to get free testnet MON.

    **On mainnet (coming soon):** VeriPay will integrate a NGN → MON onramp allowing you to buy MON with Nigerian Naira via bank transfer or mobile money.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I cancel an escrow after creating it?">
    You cannot unilaterally cancel an escrow. This protects the seller from buyers who might cancel after the seller has shipped goods. To recover funds, you must go through the [refund process](/user-guide/request-refund), which requires seller consent.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I use VeriPay on my phone?">
    Absolutely. VeriPay is fully responsive and optimized for mobile browsers. It works on Chrome, Safari, Firefox, and Brave on both iOS and Android devices.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What currencies does VeriPay support?">
    Currently, VeriPay supports **MON** (Monad's native token). Support for **USDC, USDT, and other ERC-20 tokens** is planned for v2.0. A **Naira (NGN) onramp** is planned for v1.5.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is there a minimum or maximum escrow amount?">
    There is **no minimum amount** in the smart contract — you can escrow any amount above 0. There is also **no maximum**. On testnet, you're limited by faucet availability.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Technical

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Is VeriPay open source?">
    Yes. The frontend code is fully open source on [GitHub](https://github.com/Nailer/Monad-Pay-Lagos). You can review, fork, and contribute to the codebase.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I integrate VeriPay into my own app?">
    Yes. You can interact with VeriPay's smart contract directly using the [ABI and contract address](/api-reference/smart-contract-abi). We also provide [REST API documentation](/api-reference/overview) and an [integration guide](/api-reference/integration-guide).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Who built VeriPay?">
    VeriPay is built by a developer and founder based in Lagos, Nigeria — someone who has experienced the P2P trade fraud problem firsthand and is building the solution from within the market.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
