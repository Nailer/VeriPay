## When to refund

- You can't fulfil the order (out of stock, can't deliver to their location).
- The buyer changed their mind and you're happy to cancel.
- The item was wrong and the buyer is returning it.

Refunding voluntarily is almost always better than letting a buyer report a problem: it's faster, and it **doesn't add a dispute** to your record.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the order">
    **My orders** or the **Sell** page → the order.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap Refund the buyer">
    This calls `sellerApproveRefund(id)` and emits `RefundAuthorized(id)`. VeriPay covers the network fee if needed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The buyer takes it">
    The order shows **Refund ready**. The buyer taps **Take my refund** and receives 100%.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Accordion title="In plain words" icon="lightbulb">
  You say "yes, refund". The buyer then collects their full payment. You don't pay any fee, and nothing bad goes on your record.
</Accordion>

## Things to know

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Can I undo an approval? | No. Once approved, the buyer can claim at any time while the order is open. |
| Can I refund part of an order? | Not directly. Partial outcomes happen through a dispute ruling. |
| Does it cost me a fee? | No. Refunds are never charged a fee. |
| Does it hurt my record? | Your **Paid out** doesn't increase; **Disputes** is unaffected. The buyer's **Refunds** goes up by one. |
| What if the buyer never claims it? | Once refund-approved, the order is still technically open. Note that `autoRelease` does not check `sellerApprovedRefund`, so after the window it could still pay *you*. If you've agreed a refund, remind the buyer to claim promptly. |
