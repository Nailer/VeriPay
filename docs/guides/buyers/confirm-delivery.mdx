## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the order">
    Go to **My orders** and tap the order (or open `veripay.store/trade/<id>`). Its status reads **Held safely**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspect your item first">
    Make sure it's what you paid for and it works. Once you confirm, the seller is paid immediately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap the button">
    Tap **I received my order — pay the seller**. VeriPay covers the network fee if needed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Done">
    The order shows **Completed** — *"Done — the seller has been paid. Thanks for confirming."* The seller's record gains one **Paid out**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Behind the button

The button calls `releaseToSeller(id)` on `VeriPayEscrow` from your account. The contract checks `msg.sender == buyer` and that the order is still open (`!released && !refunded`), sets `released = true`, deducts the fee locked into this order (normally 1%) into `accruedFees[token]`, transfers the remainder to the seller, and emits `FeeCharged` and `FundsReleased(id, seller)`.

<Accordion title="In plain words" icon="lightbulb">
  Only you, the buyer, can press this button. It tells the safe locker to pay the seller what they're owed (minus VeriPay's 1%), and writes a permanent public note that the order was completed.
</Accordion>

<Warning>
  **This cannot be undone.** There is no function to claw back a release. If a seller asks you to confirm *before* delivering, refuse — that removes your protection.
</Warning>

## Confirming during a dispute

If a problem was reported but you've since received the item and are satisfied, you can still confirm delivery. The contract deliberately allows `releaseToSeller` during a dispute — it's the buyer conceding, and that should never be blocked.

## If you do nothing

After the confirmation window (default 7 days from payment) the seller can be paid automatically. See [automatic release](/architecture/auto-release).
